This template is here to help you self-grade thoughtfully, not to make you fill out busywork. A good Grade Memo does three things: it checks your work honestly, it notices patterns, and it turns those patterns into one small change for next week.

**This is a metacognition tool.** That just means you are thinking about how you think and learn. Sometimes that feels clarifying. Sometimes it feels a little annoying or uncomfortable. That is normal. The goal is not to judge yourself. The goal is to notice something useful early enough to change it.

**Length target:** usually 15–25 minutes of focused work. Be concise, but specific.

* **What caused the original error?**
* **How could you have caught it earlier?**

---

## 3) What pattern are you noticing?

Look across the homework, not just at one problem.

* What kind of mistake showed up more than once?
* When does that pattern usually happen?
* What does it cost you: time, points, confidence, physical interpretation?

Try to go one level deeper than a label. "I made careless mistakes" is not a pattern. "I skip writing units once I think I know the setup, so I stop noticing when the algebra has gone off the rails" is a pattern.

---

## 4) Celebrate one small win

Name **one thing that went better** this week. It can be small.

Maybe you set up the right equation even if the algebra went sideways. Maybe your units were more consistent. Maybe you finished on time. Maybe a concept that used to feel impossible now feels manageable.

This part matters. We learn more when we notice what is starting to work.

---

## 5) What will you do differently next week?

Write **one concrete habit** for the next assignment.

Try to answer all three:

- **What will I do?**
- **When will I do it?**
- **How will I know it's working?**

Example: "After every multi-step calculation, I will pause for 30 seconds to check units and ask whether the trend makes physical sense. I'll know it's working if I stop getting backwards scaling answers."

## Rubric for the 1–5 self-grading scale

**5 — Correct + scientifically clear**  
Correct result and method. Units are consistent. Reasoning is readable. Assumptions are stated or clearly implied. The answer makes physical sense.

**4 — Essentially correct, minor issues**  
Core method and result are correct, but one minor flaw remains: a small algebra slip, a missing explanation, a formatting issue with units, or one unclear step.

**3 — Partially correct, meaningful gaps**  
Some correct reasoning is present, but there are important gaps or mistakes: incomplete setup, inconsistent units, missing explanation, or a partially correct method.

**2 — Attempted, mostly incorrect**  
A real attempt is visible, but the approach is largely incorrect or unsupported.

**1 — Minimal progress / not really scorable**  
Very little usable work is shown, or the reasoning is too incomplete to evaluate meaningfully.

**0 — No attempt**

Score based on **visible reasoning**. If the reasoning is not shown, it cannot earn a high score.
